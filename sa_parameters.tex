% Parameters of the simulated annealing for the 3DMHKP (3DMHKP-SA.py).
% Values in brackets are those of 3DMHKPup-SA.py where they differ.
\begin{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Solution representation and decoder.}
        A solution is a triple: a box sequence, an orientation preference per
        box, and a container sequence. It is mapped to a packing by an
        extreme-point decoder, so every solution is feasible by construction.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Extreme points scanned per box, \texttt{EP\_LIMIT}
                (\texttt{--ep-limit}): $0$, i.e.\ unlimited.
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Objective function.}
        Maximise the stowed value $f(S)=\sum_{j\in P(S)} v_j$ with
        $v_j = c_j \cdot \mathrm{vol}_j$ (option \texttt{--value-mode flat}
        gives $v_j = c_j$).

  \item \textbf{Initial solution $S_0$.}
        Greedy decode of the boxes in non-increasing value-density order.

  \item \textbf{Initial temperature $T_0$.}
        Not a constant: $T_0 = \gamma\, f(S_0)$ with
        $\gamma = 0.05$ (\texttt{T\_INIT\_FRACTION}, \texttt{--t-init-fraction}).
        The objective scales with the box volumes and spans
        $7.7\cdot 10^{3}$ to $2.1\cdot 10^{6}$ over the $16$ instances, so a
        single scalar $T_0$ cannot serve them all. Observed values:
        $T_0 = 384.0$ (instance~1) up to $103\,872.0$ (instance~4).
        A fixed value can be forced with \texttt{--t-init}.

  \item \textbf{Cooling schedule.}
        Geometric: $T \leftarrow \alpha\,T$.

  \item \textbf{Cooling rate $\alpha$.}
        $\alpha = 0.999$ (\texttt{ALPHA}, \texttt{--alpha}). One full sweep
        $T_0 \rightarrow T_{\min}$ takes
        $\ln(T_{\min}/T_0)/\ln\alpha \approx 12\,900$ iterations at
        $T_0 = 384$, and $\approx 18\,200$ at $T_0 = 81\,600$.

  \item \textbf{Chain length $L$.}
        $L = 1$: the temperature is lowered after \emph{every} iteration, so
        there is no inner Markov chain at fixed $T$. $L$ and $\alpha$ are
        therefore not independent; only their combination fixes the sweep
        length.

  \item \textbf{Final temperature $T_{\min}$.}
        $T_{\min} = 10^{-3}$ (\texttt{T\_MIN}, \texttt{--t-min}). A floor, not
        a stopping criterion: the run continues at $T_{\min}$ until the time
        limit.

  \item \textbf{Acceptance criterion.}
        Metropolis, for a maximisation objective with
        $\Delta = f(S') - f(S)$:
        \[
          P(\text{accept}) =
          \begin{cases}
            1, & \Delta > 0,\\[2pt]
            \exp(\Delta / T), & \Delta \le 0 .
          \end{cases}
        \]

  \item \textbf{Neighbourhood $N(S)$: move operators and their weights.}
        Relative draw probabilities per iteration (weights, normalised; a
        weight of $0$ disables the operator). Hard-coded in
        \texttt{MOVE\_WEIGHTS}, no command-line option.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item \texttt{swap} $= 1.0$ --- exchange two boxes in the sequence.
          \item \texttt{promote} $= 1.5$ --- move an unpacked box forward.
          \item \texttt{demote} $= 1.5$ $[1.0]$ --- move a packed box back.
          \item \texttt{orient} $= 1.0$ --- change one box's orientation
                preference.
          \item \texttt{container} $= 0.5$ --- reorder the container sequence;
                meaningful only on heterogeneous fleets.
          \item \texttt{relocate} $= 0.0$ --- disabled.
          \item \texttt{2opt} $= 0.0$ --- disabled.
        \end{enumerate}
        \texttt{promote} and \texttt{demote} are favoured as the only
        operators that exchange packed against unpacked cargo.

  \item \textbf{Neighbours drawn per iteration.}
        Exactly one: a single random operator and a single random move, decoded
        and then accepted or rejected. The neighbourhood is never enumerated.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Retries before a sampler gives up for the iteration,
                \texttt{\_SAMPLE\_TRIES} $= 12$; a failure is counted as a
                ``no-move draw''. Hard-coded.
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Stagnation threshold.}
        \texttt{REHEAT\_THRESHOLD} (\texttt{--reheat-threshold}), the number of
        iterations without a new \emph{global best} before the chain is
        reheated and diversified. Default
        $\max\{200,\,0.5\cdot \text{sweep}\} = 5753$.
        This single threshold triggers both the reheat (item~13) and the
        diversification (item~14); there is no separate diversification
        threshold. Observed frequency: $82$ to $1150$ triggers per instance in
        a $1800$\,s run.

  \item \textbf{Reheat factor.}
        \texttt{REHEAT\_FRACTION} $= 0.25$ (\texttt{--reheat-fraction}). On a
        trigger the temperature is \emph{reset},
        $T \leftarrow 0.25\,T_0$, not scaled from its current value: after a
        long stagnation $T$ has annealed close to $T_{\min}$, where a
        multiplicative reheat would have no effect.

  \item \textbf{Diversification strength.}
        The perturbation applied to the current solution at the same trigger
        as the reheat. Two properties frame it. It is applied
        \emph{unconditionally}: the kicked solution replaces the current one
        without a Metropolis test, so the chain is displaced whether or not
        the kick was good. And it acts only on the current solution, never on
        the incumbent best, so a destructive kick cannot cost the run its
        result --- the risk it carries is spent search time, not lost value.
        Let $n$ be the number of boxes and $U(S)$ the set the decoder left
        unplaced. The kick has three magnitudes, all hard-coded, with no
        command-line option:
        \begin{enumerate}

          \item \emph{Promotion of rejected cargo.}
                $m = \max\{1, \lfloor |U(S)|/3 \rfloor\}$ of the unplaced
                boxes are drawn uniformly without replacement, removed from
                the sequence, and each reinserted at a position drawn
                uniformly from $\{0,\dots,h\}$ with
                $h = \max\{1, \lfloor n/3 \rfloor\}$. The insertions are
                sequential and every index is drawn against the same constant
                $h$, so all $m$ boxes come to lie within the first $h+m$
                positions. The step is skipped entirely when
                $U(S) = \emptyset$. Because the decoder is order-greedy, a
                promoted box gets first claim on the container space and
                displaces whatever previously occupied it; this is the
                same exchange of packed against unpacked cargo as the
                \texttt{promote} operator, but performed on $m$ boxes at once
                instead of one.

          \item \emph{Block scramble.} If $n \ge 4$, a contiguous window of
                length $s = \max\{2, \lfloor n/5 \rfloor\}$ is chosen at a
                uniformly random start position $i \in \{0,\dots,n-s\}$ and
                its entries are uniformly shuffled. It is applied to the
                sequence \emph{after} the promotion step. This reorders a
                block in one step, where the \texttt{swap} operator would need
                many accepted moves to reach the same permutation.

          \item \emph{Container sequence.} The container order is shuffled
                unconditionally, which changes the order in which containers
                are filled. Containers are filled one at a time, so the first
                in the order has first claim on the whole cargo pool and the
                last sees only what the others left; the shuffle therefore
                decides which containers receive the high-value cargo. Unlike
                the \texttt{container} move operator it does not skip
                permutations of same-type containers, which decode to an
                identical packing. All 16 instances have heterogeneous fleets
                (2 to 3 container types over 2 to 15 containers), so a shuffle
                is usually effective, though on the smallest fleets a
                substantial share of draws is the identity.

        \end{enumerate}
        The solution is then re-decoded in place, so the reheated chain
        continues from the perturbed packing.

        The magnitudes are fractions of $n$ and $|U(S)|$, hence instance-
        dependent but fixed relative to size. Over the 16 instances
        $n$ ranges from 47 to 200, giving an insertion window $h$ of 15 to 66
        and a scrambled block $s$ of 9 to 40; the number of promoted boxes $m$
        also depends on how much cargo the current solution rejects, and in
        the $1800$\,s runs ranged from 3 (instance~9, 9 boxes unplaced) to 53
        (instance~10, 160 unplaced). Instance~13 packs all 84 boxes, so its
        promotion step is inactive.

        Two consequences are worth stating. First, the strength does not adapt
        to how long the search has been stuck: the same kick is applied at the
        first stagnation and at the thousandth. Second, since the trigger
        fires between 82 and 1150 times in an $1800$\,s run, the kick is not a
        rare restart but a routine part of the search, and its magnitude is
        arguably a more sensitive tuning parameter than the reheat factor
        itself. Unlike the cooling rate and the reheat factor it is not
        exposed on the command line, so varying it requires editing the
        source.

  \item \textbf{Stopping criterion.}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Wall-clock limit per instance, \texttt{TIME\_LIMIT}
                (\texttt{--time-limit}): $1800$\,s $[300$\,s$]$. This is the
                binding criterion in all runs reported here.
          \item Iteration cap, \texttt{MAX\_ITERATIONS}
                (\texttt{--max-iterations}): $5\cdot 10^{8}$
                $[5\cdot 10^{7}]$; never reached, as a $1800$\,s run performs
                between $0.9$ and $6.6$ million iterations depending on the
                instance ($477$ to $3656$ iterations per second).
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Random seed.}
        \texttt{--seed}; drawn at random when not given, and seeded once per
        process.

\end{enumerate}
